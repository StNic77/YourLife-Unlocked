## 2. What Was Completed

### The Sanctuary Prompt Brief — Locked
`YourLifeUnlocked_Sanctuary_Prompt_Brief.md`

The Brief moved from Draft to Final this session. Section 12 — previously an open items list — was replaced with a fully written section covering the 'not ready' response, the ATAK tile, and the two-response model for active decline versus passive non-response.

**The ATAK tile — locked:**

> *"Ready to slow down for a minute?"*

This is the door to the Sanctuary in the ATAK. The word "Sanctuary" never appears outside the room. The tile asks without pressing. "Ready" makes not-yet a completely valid answer. "For a minute" makes the commitment small. It is the opening sequence's Move One applied before the user has even entered.

**The two-response model — locked:**

- **Active decline ("I don't want this")** — respected as a boundary. The Sanctuary stays available in the ATAK but stops resurfacing actively. SHAPE notes the decline. The door remains open. The knocking stops.
- **Passive non-response ("not right now")** — treated as timing, not rejection. SHAPE watches condition, not elapsed time. If the load persists, deepens, or new signals emerge, the Sanctuary resurfaces. Not urgently. Not repeatedly. But it tries again, because the hard season is still real.

**The resurfacing is part of the tending.** The Sanctuary appearing a second time, a third time, does something the first offer could not. The person sees it and thinks: *this keeps showing up. Maybe there's something to that.* They may not enter. But something has shifted. The persistence of the offer, without pressure, is itself a form of care. The user does not have to enter the Sanctuary for the resurfacing to have done its work.

All decisions in Section 12 carry the caveat: current best thinking, subject to evolution through deployment.

### Six World Opening Sequences — Locked as Direction

The Sanctuary Prompt Brief specified that the opening sequence moves differently in every world but follows the same architecture. Section 4 of the Brief said *"the exact words belong to the world the user inhabits."* This session wrote those words for six of the eight worlds.

The sequences are locked as direction. Polish pass comes later, in context, not before. The bones are right.

**The three moves — restated for the record:**

**Move One — The Offer.** The subject is the app, not the user. Opens a door, makes no demands. So light it barely lands. Not-yet is a completely valid answer.

**Move Two — The Glimpse.** Names the trigger — the pattern SHAPE has been watching. Names the final blow — the specific moment it broke. Two things only. Plain, no drama. Seen, not caught. The pile is never named. The person is the point.

**Move Three — The Permission Ask.** Hands control immediately back to the user. Then silence. The AI said the thing. It waits.

## 3. The Six World Sequences

### The Operator
> *"Hey. Get out of your own way for a minute?"*
> *"You've been holding a lot of fronts. One of them just got harder to hold."*
> *"Do you want to work the problem?"*

**How it was found:** "Get out of your own way" came from Shawn before any candidate lines were offered. The dark humour jostle that an operator would respect. Move Three — "work the problem" — was confirmed against the teammate distinction: the Operator is a stoic teammate, not a stoic independent. He receives care through directness and shared purpose. The question doesn't hand control back tentatively — it assumes they're both in the room and asks if they're doing this. That's respect, not pressure.

**Flag for polish pass:** "Fronts" in Move Two sits costume-adjacent. Not costume — the meaning carries without the gear. But it's the closest thing to a uniform in any of the twelve lines. Worth one look in the polish pass.

---

### The Range
> *"Want to walk back and look at it?"*
> *"You've been pushing through this for a while. It's starting to cost."*
> *"Worth talking through?"*

**How it was found:** Move One was sharpened mid-session. The original candidate — *"Want to stop and look at this for a minute?"* — passed the initial tests but failed the honest read. "Look at this" pointed at something unnamed. The Range person deserved the same precision the Operator got.

The sharpening came from Shawn describing his father — Alberta farmer, harvest machines breaking, getting down off the tractor, walking back to the broken piece of machinery, surveying it, developing a plan, executing. An OODA loop running quietly in a field with nobody watching. The gesture is the Range person doing what they already know how to do. "Walk back" is the tractor without the tractor. The image is universal. The meaning carries.

The distinction that locked Move Three: the Range person is a stoic independent. "Worth talking through?" leaves all the space. No teammate energy. No shared purpose assumed. Just — the question, and then silence.

---

### The Garden
> *"Everything's taken care of. Can we talk about you for a minute?"*
> *"Everyone around you is well tended. You're the one who's been missed."*
> *"Can we tend to you for a little while?"*

**How it was found:** The guilt of receiving — named in Session 31 as the key that unlocks this world — governed every decision. The Garden person won't hear the question until she knows everyone else is okay. Move One carries the reassurance before anything is asked. The "we" reduces the burden — she doesn't have to receive alone.

Move Two is the strongest single line across all twelve. "You're the one who's been missed" names the absence without blame. "Missed" carries warmth that "overlooked" or "neglected" never could. The kudos lands first — the giving is seen — and then the absence is named so softly it doesn't accuse. Seen, not caught. Tended, not diagnosed.

Move Three turns the act of receiving into something she already knows how to do. She's not being asked to do something foreign. She's being asked to tend — just this once, in a different direction. "Can we tend to you for a little while?" The "we" makes it shared. "For a little while" removes the clock. She doesn't have to make herself the priority permanently. Just for a little while.

Note on "tend": the word appears in Move Two ("well tended") and Move Three ("tend to you"). This was considered. The judgment is resonance over repetition — the word the Garden person understands most deeply, used first to describe what she gives and then what she deserves.

---

### The Journey
> *"Want to check you're still on the path you chose?"*
> *"You've been moving fast on ground that asks you to slow down."*
> *"Shall we look at where we're stepping next?"*

**How it was found:** Move One was sharpened after the honest assessment mid-session. The original — *"Want to slow down and make sure you're on the path you chose?"* — repeated "slow down" against Move Two. The repetition was the problem, not the instinct. The fix: "check" replaced "slow down." A check isn't a stop. It's a purposeful moment of verification before the next move. Native to how the Journey person operates.

"The path you chose" carries the agency. The question doesn't imply they're lost or wrong. It asks: is this still the one you chose? For the shadow Journey person — running away rather than toward — the answer forming before they've tapped anything is doing the work.

Move Two: the ground is the authority, not the app. "The ground that asks you to slow down" removes judgment from the product and places the logic where it belongs — in the reality of the terrain. A sane person on a ridgeline slows down. This person hasn't.

The image that unlocked it: a Hollywood movie ridgeline pass, barely carved into the side of a cliff, thousands of feet of drop. The danger isn't the path — the Journey person may have chosen a dangerous path deliberately. The danger is the speed on that path. SHAPE isn't watching the path. It's watching the pace.

Move Three: "Shall we look at where we're stepping next?" — the handhold. Motion assumed, not granted. The Journey person is not being asked to stop. The "shall we" has a gentleness that "want to" doesn't. A fellow traveler already alongside, reaching out a hand. The "we" here is a companion on the ridge, not a teammate or a shared burden. Someone who sees the exposure and offers to navigate the next stretch together.

### The Practice
> *"The world leaking in lately?"*
> *"The thing that clears it for you. It's not clearing."*
> *"Want to look at what's getting in?"*

**How it was found:** The hardest world to unlock in this session. Required Shawn to place himself — on the mountain bike, on the snowboard, on the morning walk — and ask honestly: what does the Sanctuary offer this person that the practice doesn't already give them?

The answer: nothing new. The practice was the peace before the app had a Sanctuary. When the practice breaks down — when the container can no longer hold what's in it — the Sanctuary isn't offering a replacement. It's offering to sit with the person in the place where the thing that used to work no longer does.

The critical insight: it is not the practice that is failing. It is external forces — financial pressure, a series of life problems, a medical issue, a family difficulty — that have exceeded what any practice can clear. The practice is the signal. The load is the cause. SHAPE reads the signal. The Sanctuary addresses the load.

This principle applies to every world. The practice session is where it became most explicit. The practice isn't broken. The world got in.

"The world leaking in" came from Shawn before any candidate was offered. It is the most precise description of what the Practice person experiences when the container breaks. Not an invasion. A leak. Something that was sealed is no longer sealed.

Move Two names the function, not the practice. "The thing that clears it for you" respects the privacy of the practice completely — it could be a mountain bike, a morning walk, a meditation cushion, a run. The app isn't naming it. It's naming what it does. And then naming what it's failing to do. The period between the two sentences is a beat — a moment of recognition before the second sentence lands.

Move Three: "Want to look at what's getting in?" — plain, direct, respects the self-awareness of the Practice person. They already know something is getting in. Move One named it. The question is only: do you want to look at it together?

**Important note that emerged from this world:** The world is a language register, not a setting. The moment the session started drifting toward the imagery — the mountain bike, the trail, the clearing — Shawn pulled back to the person every time. That discipline is visible in the three lines. The practice is never named. The person is always the point. This discipline applies to every world, every piece of copy, every cascade. The image is the container. The person is the content.

### The Meadow
> *"We've been here before. And we found our way through."*
> *"You keep coming back to the same thing. It hasn't resolved."*
> *"Want to find our way back to solid ground?"*

**How it was found:** The hardest sequence of the session. Required Shawn to put himself on the page honestly — the relationship that eroded him gradually, the long work of opening every drawer and door in his mind and inspecting every object, the finding of the meadow, and then the return of the old threat that shook the ground again.

The Meadow hard season is not a new loss. It is the return of the old threat. The specific terror of it: the Meadow person did everything right. They did the work. They know themselves now. And the ground shook anyway.

The most profound insight of the session — and possibly of the project — came in the writing of Move Three.

**The "we" in the Meadow is the user and themselves across time.**

Not the app and the user. Not a teammate. Not a fellow traveler. The person they were and the person they became after the hard road. The Sanctuary steps into that conversation — the conversation the Meadow person has already been having with themselves, the one that sounds like *thank you for surviving, it's okay, look at me now, things will get better* — and holds the space for it to continue.

The Sanctuary in the Meadow world isn't a caring watchful friend from the outside. It's holding up a mirror so the Meadow person can hear the voice they already have. The one that took the hard road and came out the other side.

Move One: "We've been here before. And we found our way through." — the tone is everything. Not an indictment. Not a warning. A reassurance. Said the way Shawn said *look at me now, things will get better* to a younger version of himself. Quiet confidence. Not dismissing the difficulty. Not minimizing the shaking ground. Just — we know this. We've stood here. We're still standing. The proof is you. You're in the meadow. The road through is real because you already walked it.

Move Two: SHAPE reads the signals around the practice, not the practice itself. The Meadow person copes well. They have tools. They reach for the right things when the ground shakes — more rides, more time with friends, more conversations, more entries into the reflecting pool. From the outside they look fine. The signal SHAPE is looking for is the asymmetry of effort: not dysfunction, but overcompensation. The quiet grinding of someone working very hard to stay well.

For the Meadow person, the richest signal is the reflecting pool — whatever we ultimately name that domain. The thread they keep returning to. The thing that hasn't resolved. SHAPE sees the return visits. The Glimpse names what it found: *"You keep coming back to the same thing. It hasn't resolved."* No drama. No pile. Just the accurate observation of something that has been watching and noticed the thread.

Move Three: "Want to find our way back to solid ground?" — the "we" carries the shared history across time. "Solid ground" names the destination without naming the journey. The person knows exactly what that means. They've stood there. And the question carries hope without promising anything — because the Meadow person already knows the ground exists. They found it once. The Sanctuary is offering to help them find their way back to it.

## 4. The Architectural Doors Opened — Critical

This section is the most important part of this handoff. The session opened three architectural doors that must not be built behind until each has its own dedicated thinking session. They are named here in full because they represent the next phase of the product's deepest work.

---

### Door One — The Reflecting Pool / "Has Something Changed?" Evolution

**What was named:**

The "Has something changed?" domain needs to evolve into something more than a capture mechanism. The current framing — a question that invites the user to note life events — is correct in instinct but insufficient in architecture.

What it needs to become: a reflecting pool. A private space where the user can speak freely — process, vent, grieve, celebrate, confuse themselves out loud — knowing it stays inside the app and never leaves. Not journaling into nothing. Not data entry into a form. A conversation with something that is paying attention.

**The dual function:**

For the user: permission to speak into the void. Private. Safe. No judgment. No action items. No list of next steps. Just space. The user who needs to say something they've never said to another person has somewhere to say it.

For SHAPE: the richest intelligence feed in the entire app. Not domain data or behavioral signals alone — actual language. Threads. Patterns. What the person returns to. What they can't resolve. What they circle back to three weeks later still unresolved. SHAPE reads this. The ATAK receives it as part of the fused picture.

**The trust architecture:** The user doesn't need to know it's feeding SHAPE. They need to know it's private and it matters. The intelligence happens underneath. The Sanctuary surfaces when SHAPE can't hold the load alone.

**This reframe also answers the Meadow SHAPE question.** The reflecting pool is where the Meadow person's unresolved thread lives. SHAPE sees the return visits. The Glimpse names what it found there.

**Status:** Requires a dedicated thinking session. Do not touch this domain until that session has happened. The name, the collection discipline, the HUMINT principles — all of it needs to be defined before a single line of that domain is written.

---

### Door Two — HUMINT Collection Principles as Governing Discipline

**What was named:**

The reflecting pool isn't just a journal. It is a HUMINT collection point. And the domain that houses it needs to be built on human intelligence collection principles — not to assess the worth of the source, but to use the real extractive process to answer intelligence requirements.

The intelligence requirements belong to the commander. The commander is the user. The user doesn't know their intelligence requirements. The app's job is to answer them anyway — by creating the conditions for the user to say things they didn't know they needed to say.

The HUMINT collector doesn't ask "what's bothering you." They ask the question that makes the source feel safe enough to say the thing they've never said out loud. And then they ask the next one. The thread emerges not because the collector demanded it but because the conditions were right.

For the user: it feels like being heard. Like a conversation that went somewhere real. Like something actually understood them.

For SHAPE: it is structured collection discipline. Requirements driven. The questions working toward the intelligence picture the ATAK needs to do its job.

The world voice guide runs underneath all of it. The Operator gets collected differently than the Meadow person. Same discipline. Different register. The battlespace is the user. SHAPE is the operator in the field. The extraction is always in service of understanding the battlespace more completely.

**Status:** Requires a dedicated thinking session. The HUMINT principles, the extraction process, the intelligence requirements framework — all must be defined before build. This session is likely the most important thinking session remaining on the product.

---

### Door Three — The ATAK / SHAPE / Sanctuary Architecture — Corrected and Final

**What was clarified — this is now the authoritative architecture statement:**

**The Reflecting Pool / SHAPE Domain** — the collection point. Where the user speaks freely. Where the raw human intelligence lives. Unstructured, emotional, unfiltered. The richest data in the system.

**SHAPE** — the human intelligence operator. Not a domain. Not a tile. No click. Always running. Always watching. Reads the reflecting pool. Watches behavioral signals across all domains. Identifies patterns, threads, asymmetries. Processes the human picture continuously and invisibly. Delivers that intelligence into the ATAK as the human layer on top of the cold domain data.

SHAPE is not the all-source intelligence cell. SHAPE is the human intelligence operator feeding that cell.

**SHAPE makes it human.** That is the line. That is what separates this product from every other intelligence layer ever built.

**ATAK** — the all-source fusion cell. Receives everything. Domain data — schedule, health, vehicles, maintenance, calendar. And SHAPE's human intelligence feed. Fuses it all into the complete picture. The cold brief made whole by what SHAPE brings in. The ATAK sees the whole battlespace and presents it. It is the intelligence product.

**The Sanctuary** — opens when the fused picture — the cold domains plus SHAPE's human layer — exceeds what data and intelligence alone can address. When the person needs more than the ATAK can show them. Not a domain. Not a tile in the normal sense. A room that opens when the human picture requires it.

**The flow:**

*User speaks into the reflecting pool → SHAPE reads it → SHAPE watches all domain signals → SHAPE feeds the ATAK → ATAK fuses everything → Sanctuary opens when the load demands it.*

**The design requirement that governs all of this:**

> *If this thing is going to work, SHAPE has to be a superhero.*

This is not a metaphor. It is a design standard. SHAPE running at half-capacity produces a product that feels like surveillance. SHAPE running as designed produces a product that feels like it genuinely knows you. Everything else in the architecture depends on SHAPE doing its job at the highest possible level of sophistication.

**Status:** This architecture correction must travel into the SHAPE Definition Document, the ATAK Definition Document, and the Rules and Guidance before the next build session. It supersedes anything written before it on the relationship between these three layers.
