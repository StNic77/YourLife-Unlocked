No code was written. Five documents were produced or updated. Several architectural doors were opened that will require their own dedicated sessions before anything gets built behind them.

## 2. What Was Completed

### The Sanctuary Prompt Brief — Final
`YourLifeUnlocked_Sanctuary_Prompt_Brief.md`

The Brief is Final. Fourteen sections. All eight world opening sequences written, assessed, and appended as Section 13. The document's standing section renumbered to Section 14.

**The ATAK tile — locked:**

> *"Ready to slow down for a minute?"*

This is the door to the Sanctuary in the ATAK. The word "Sanctuary" never appears outside the room. The tile asks without pressing. "Ready" makes not-yet a completely valid answer. "For a minute" makes the commitment small. It is the opening sequence's Move One applied before the user has even entered.

**The two-response model — locked:**

- **Active decline ("I don't want this")** — respected as a boundary. The Sanctuary stays available in the ATAK but stops resurfacing actively. SHAPE notes the decline. The door remains open. The knocking stops.
- **Passive non-response ("not right now")** — treated as timing, not rejection. SHAPE watches condition, not elapsed time. If the load persists, deepens, or new signals emerge, the Sanctuary resurfaces. Not urgently. Not repeatedly. But it tries again, because the hard season is still real.

**The resurfacing is part of the tending.** The Sanctuary appearing a second time, a third time, does something the first offer could not. The person sees it and thinks: *this keeps showing up. Maybe there's something to that.* They may not enter. But something has shifted. The persistence of the offer, without pressure, is itself a form of care. The user does not have to enter the Sanctuary for the resurfacing to have done its work.

All decisions carry the caveat: current best thinking, subject to evolution through deployment.

### All Eight World Opening Sequences — Locked as Direction

The three moves are identical in architecture across all eight worlds. Only the register changes.

**Move One — The Offer.** Subject is the app, not the user. Opens a door, makes no demands. So light it barely lands. Not-yet is a completely valid answer.

**Move Two — The Glimpse.** Names the trigger and the final blow. Two things only. Plain, no drama. Seen, not caught. The pile is never named. The person is the point.

**Move Three — The Permission Ask.** Hands control immediately back to the user. Then silence.

## 3. The Eight World Sequences — With How Each Was Found

### The Operator
> *"Hey. Get out of your own way for a minute?"*
> *"You've been holding a lot of fronts. One of them just got harder to hold."*
> *"Do you want to work the problem?"*

Move One came from Shawn before any candidate lines were offered — the dark humour jostle a teammate would respect. The key distinction that locked Move Three: the Operator is a stoic teammate, not a stoic independent. "Do you want to work the problem?" doesn't hand control back tentatively — it assumes they're both in the room and asks if they're doing this. That's respect, not pressure.

**Polish pass flag:** "Fronts" in Move Two sits costume-adjacent. Reviewed and held intentionally — it earns its place for this world. Confirm in context.

---

### The Range
> *"Want to walk back and look at it?"*
> *"You've been pushing through this for a while. It's starting to cost."*
> *"Worth talking through?"*

Move One was sharpened mid-session. The original — *"Want to stop and look at this for a minute?"* — failed the honest read. The sharpening came from Shawn describing his father — Alberta farmer, harvest machines breaking, getting down off the tractor, walking back to the broken piece, surveying it, developing a plan, executing. An OODA loop running quietly in a field with nobody watching. "Walk back" is the tractor without the tractor. The image is universal. The meaning carries without the gear.

The distinction that locked Move Three: the Range person is a stoic independent. "Worth talking through?" leaves all the space. No teammate energy. No shared purpose assumed. Just the question, and then silence.

---

### The Garden
> *"Everything's taken care of. Can we talk about you for a minute?"*
> *"Everyone around you is well tended. You're the one who's been missed."*
> *"Can we tend to you for a little while?"*

The guilt of receiving governed every decision. The Garden person won't hear the question until she knows everyone else is okay. Move One carries the reassurance before anything is asked. The "we" reduces the burden — she doesn't have to receive alone.

Move Two is the strongest single line across all twenty-four. "You're the one who's been missed" names the absence without blame. "Missed" carries warmth that "overlooked" or "neglected" never could. The kudos lands first. Then the absence, so softly it doesn't accuse. Seen, not caught. Tended, not diagnosed.

Move Three turns the act of receiving into something she already knows how to do — tend — just this once, in a different direction. The "we" makes it shared. "For a little while" removes the clock.

**Polish pass flag:** "Tend/tended" appears in Move Two and Move Three. Almost certainly resonance — the word the Garden person understands most deeply, used first to name what she gives and then what she deserves. Confirm in context.

---

### The Journey
> *"Want to check you're still on the path you chose?"*
> *"You've been moving fast on ground that asks you to slow down."*
> *"Shall we look at where we're stepping next?"*

Move One was sharpened after the honest assessment. The original repeated "slow down" against Move Two — the repetition was the problem, not the instinct. "Check" replaced it. A check isn't a stop. It's a purposeful moment of verification before the next move. Native to how the Journey person operates.

"The path you chose" carries the agency. The question doesn't imply they're lost. It asks: is this still the one you chose? The image that unlocked Move Two: a ridgeline pass barely carved into a cliff face — the danger isn't the path, it's the speed on it. A sane person slows down on that ground. This person hasn't.

Move Three: "Shall we look at where we're stepping next?" — the handhold. Motion assumed, not granted. The "shall we" has a gentleness that "want to" doesn't. A fellow traveler already alongside, reaching out a hand.

### The Practice
> *"The world leaking in lately?"*
> *"The thing that clears it for you. It's not clearing."*
> *"Want to look at what's getting in?"*

The hardest world to unlock in this session. Required Shawn to place himself — on the mountain bike, on the snowboard, on the morning walk — and ask honestly: what does the Sanctuary offer this person that the practice doesn't already give them?

The critical insight that unlocked everything: it is not the practice that is failing. External forces — financial pressure, a series of life problems, a medical issue, a family difficulty — have exceeded what any practice can clear. The practice is the signal. The load is the cause. This principle applies to every world. The Practice session is where it became most explicit.

"The world leaking in" came from Shawn before any candidate was offered. Move Two names the function, not the practice — "the thing that clears it" respects that every Practice person's practice is different. The period between the two sentences is a beat — a moment of recognition before the second sentence lands.

**The discipline named here that governs all eight worlds:** The world is a register, not a setting. The moment the language drifts toward imagery — the costume, the tractor, the ridgeline, the mountain bike — pull back to the person. The person is always the source. The image is just how SHAPE knew which register to use.

### The Meadow
> *"We've been here before. And we found our way through."*
> *"You keep coming back to the same thing. It hasn't resolved."*
> *"Want to find our way back to solid ground?"*

The hardest sequence of the session. Required Shawn to put himself on the page — the relationship that eroded him gradually, the long work of opening every drawer and door in his mind, the finding of the meadow, and then the return of the old threat that shook the ground again.

The Meadow hard season is not a new loss. It is the return of the old threat. The specific terror: the Meadow person did everything right. They did the work. They know themselves. And the ground shook anyway.

**The most profound insight of the session — and possibly of the project:**

> *The "we" in the Meadow is the user and themselves across time.*

Not the app and the user. Not a teammate. Not a fellow traveler. The person they were and the person they became after the hard road. The Sanctuary steps into the conversation the Meadow person has already been having with themselves — the one that sounds like *thank you for surviving, it's okay, look at me now, things will get better* — and holds the space for it to continue.

The Sanctuary in the Meadow world isn't a caring watchful friend from the outside. It's holding up a mirror so the Meadow person can hear the voice they already have.

Move Two names what SHAPE can actually see — the thread the person keeps returning to in the reflecting pool. The return visits are the signal. "You keep coming back to the same thing. It hasn't resolved." No drama. No pile. Just the accurate observation of something that has been watching.

### The Playbook
> *"Been a while since someone kept up?"*
> *"You've been winning. But there's nobody to tell."*
> *"Want to figure out what you're actually chasing?"*

The person so intent on winning they've moved so far ahead that the room emptied without them noticing. Shawn located this world in his own early-to-mid twenties — needing to be the smartest person in the room, not understanding for a long time why that kept making the room quieter.

Move One is generous — it honors the speed without indicting it. "Been a while since someone kept up?" sees the capability, not the cost.

Move Two is the second strongest single line across all twenty-four. "You've been winning. But there's nobody to tell." The achievement seen first. The loneliness second. No blame. The period between the two sentences doing everything.

Move Three reorients without shaming. "Want to figure out what you're actually chasing?" — the "actually" implies they haven't looked at this honestly, without announcing it. The Sanctuary never indicts the drive. It just asks whether the drive is pointed at the right thing.

---

### The Summit
> *"You got there. Was it what you thought it would be?"*
> *"You keep getting there. Something's been missing when you do."*
> *"Want to figure out how to make the next one feel full?"*

The person who competes with the terrain and themselves. The hard season: arrival without witness. They got there. The mountain didn't disappoint them. The solitude did. Shawn named this from his own experience — doing the hard hike, reaching the summit, surveying the view, thinking *okay, I've seen it, I'm off* — instead of the deep joy that exact same place would have given with someone standing beside him.

Move One: "You got there. Was it what you thought it would be?" — no mountain, no terrain, no costume. Genuinely open. The question doesn't assume the answer.

Move Three honors the drive completely. The next climb is assumed. The Sanctuary never shames the Summit person for being who they are. "Want to figure out how to make the next one feel full?" adds the missing dimension without issuing a verdict on the ones that came before.

**Polish pass flag:** "Getting there" in Move Two echoes "got there" in Move One. One word to look at.

## 5. The Architectural Doors Opened — Critical

This section is the most important part of this handoff. Three architectural doors were opened this session that must not be built behind until each has its own dedicated thinking session.

---

### Door One — The Reflecting Pool / "Has Something Changed?" Evolution

"Has something changed?" needs to evolve into something more than a capture mechanism. Its destination is a **reflecting pool** — a private space where the user can speak freely: process, vent, grieve, celebrate, confuse themselves out loud, say things they have never said to another person.

**The dual function:**

For the user: permission to speak into the void. Private. Safe. No judgment. No action items.

For SHAPE: the richest intelligence feed in the entire app. Actual language. Threads. What the person returns to. What they can't resolve. SHAPE reads this and feeds what it finds into the ATAK as the human layer on top of the cold domain data.

The user doesn't need to know it's feeding SHAPE. They need to know it's private and it matters. The intelligence happens underneath.

**Status:** Requires a dedicated thinking session before build. Name, collection discipline, UI, trust architecture — all must be defined first. Do not build before that session has happened.

---

### Door Two — HUMINT Collection Principles as Governing Discipline

The reflecting pool is not a passive journal. It uses human intelligence collection principles to draw depth from the user in a way that feels like conversation, not interrogation.

The intelligence requirements belong to the commander. The commander is the user. The user doesn't know their intelligence requirements. The app answers them anyway — by creating conditions for the user to say things they didn't know they needed to say.

The HUMINT collector doesn't ask "what's bothering you." They ask the question that makes the source feel safe enough to say the thing they've never said out loud. And then they ask the next one.

The world voice guide runs underneath. The Operator is collected differently than the Meadow person. Same discipline. Different register. The battlespace is the user. SHAPE is the operator in the field.

**Status:** Requires its own dedicated thinking session. Likely the most important thinking session remaining on the product.

---

### Door Three — The ATAK / SHAPE / Sanctuary Architecture — Corrected and Final

**This is now the authoritative architecture statement. It supersedes anything written before it.**

**The Reflecting Pool / SHAPE Domain** — the collection point. Raw human intelligence. Unstructured, emotional, unfiltered.

**SHAPE** — the human intelligence operator. Not a domain. Not a tile. No click. Always running. Reads the reflecting pool. Watches behavioral signals. Delivers human intelligence into the ATAK. SHAPE does not produce the brief. SHAPE is not the all-source cell. SHAPE is the operator in the field feeding that cell.

**ATAK** — the all-source fusion cell. Receives everything — cold domain data and SHAPE's human intelligence feed. Fuses it all. Produces the intelligence product.

**The Sanctuary** — opens when the fused picture exceeds what data and intelligence alone can address.

**The flow:**
*User speaks into the reflecting pool → SHAPE reads it → SHAPE watches all domain signals → SHAPE feeds the ATAK → ATAK fuses everything → Sanctuary opens when the load demands it.*

**The design standard:**

> *If this thing is going to work, SHAPE has to be a superhero.*

This is not a metaphor. It is a design standard. SHAPE running at half-capacity produces a product that feels like surveillance. SHAPE running as designed produces a product that feels like it genuinely knows you. Everything else depends on SHAPE doing its job at the highest possible level of sophistication.
