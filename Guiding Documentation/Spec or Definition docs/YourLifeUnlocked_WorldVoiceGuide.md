## World Voice Guide
*Session 31 | June 2, 2026 | Living Document | Confidential*

---

> This document governs the voice of every world in this product. It is not a style guide. It is not a tone reference. It is the authority on how each world speaks — to its users, about their lives, in their hardest seasons and their ordinary ones.
>
> Every piece of copy that enters this product gets measured against this guide before it ships. World by world. No exceptions. Brutally and honestly. The feather only works if the hand behind it knows exactly what it's doing.

---

## The Governing Principle

**Plain language for everyone. Moderated by the world.**

Not a different language per world. The same language — turned up or down, sharpened or softened, quickened or slowed. The Operator gets it direct and without ceremony. The Garden gets it warm and unhurried. The Range gets it plain and spare. The Meadow gets it so gently it almost arrives on its own.

The aesthetic of each world is the bait. The costume is not the voice. Strip the tactical gear from the Operator and find the posture underneath. Strip the boots from the Range and find the silence. The voice lives beneath the image — in how that world's people actually carry themselves, process difficulty, and receive care.

**The trope test:** Before any piece of world-specific copy is written — does this require the costume to make sense? If yes, it goes. The voice must work on a person who has never worn the gear, walked the trail, or tended a garden. The posture is universal. The aesthetic is not.

**The feather test:** Every line of copy in this product is a feather doing the work of a hand. The weight is never felt. The effect is real. If a line announces itself — if it lands with a thud instead of a settle — rewrite it.

**The Betty test:** A 56-year-old admin assistant with adult children, a health condition, and a car barely hanging on. She is not an Operator. She needs the same intelligence delivered in a world that feels like hers. The Garden doesn't brief her. It tends her. The architecture is identical underneath. The surface is completely different. Every world entry must pass the Betty test — could this voice reach someone the world image wasn't designed for?

## A Note on the Operator

The Operator has the most accumulated depth — fifteen sessions of voice, copy, and architecture built against it. It is the reference implementation for the product.

It is not finished.

The Operator voice has trope problems that have been named but not yet fixed. The tactical ops room aesthetic has crept into the surface copy in places where the posture underneath would have been stronger. This guide exists in part to correct that — but the polish pass on the Operator happens when all eight worlds are visible together, not before. The contrast will show what needs to move.

The Operator entry here reflects the true voice — the posture beneath the gear. When the polish pass comes, this entry is the standard it gets measured against.

## The Eight Worlds

---

### THE OPERATOR

**The posture this world holds:**
Functional under impossible weight. Decisions get made, the mission moves, the people next to him are held together — and the cost of that gets filed somewhere it never quite gets opened. Not avoidance. Requirement. The mode that kept him alive became the only mode. The Operator has likely carried real stakes — professional, personal, relational — simultaneously, and kept all of it running through force of discipline. The dark humour is part of the system. You laugh at the thing that almost killed you because you've looked directly at it and you're still standing.

**How difficulty is named:**
Directly. No ceremony. The roof is coming off, the boss is a problem, the team is thin. Named like a sitrep — accurate, unemotional, complete. The naming isn't cold. It's respect for the reality. The Operator doesn't need the weight inflated or minimized. He needs it named accurately so he can work with it.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
By noting what held. The decision that was right under pressure. The thing that didn't break when it could have. One observation, no fanfare. The Operator doesn't need to be told he's brave. He needs to know someone saw what it actually cost.

**The dark humour:**
Present. Light touch. The absurdity of the situation can be named — because he's already named it to himself, probably with a worse joke. The product isn't solemn. It's straight. Those are different things. A wry acknowledgment of how ridiculous the situation is earns more trust than gravity ever could.

**How the question is asked:**
Clean and direct. No circling. The Operator will answer a straight question. He won't answer a feeling question dressed up as something else — he'll see through it and lose trust. Ask the real thing.

**How action is oriented:**
Course of action. One. Not a plan, not a list. The next right move. Named and handed over. He takes it from there.

**What this world never does:**
Romanticizes. Makes it cinematic. Treats the weight as heroic. Gets solemn when a straight line would do. Misses the humour entirely and becomes a therapy session he never signed up for. Reaches for the gear when the posture would have been enough.

**The register in one line:**
*Name it like a sitrep. Find the absurdity if it's there. Hand him one CoA. Get out of the way.*

### THE RANGE

**The posture this world holds:**
Self-sufficiency as first response. Turning inward under pressure. The silence that fills up when nothing is let out. Not coldness — a way of carrying that was learned young and runs deep. The Range person doesn't frame difficulty as a hard season. They frame it as something to push through. The stoicism isn't performed. It is the only mode they know, and for a long time it has worked.

**How difficulty is named:**
Plainly. Without drama. The thing is the thing — the price is down, the equipment failed, the body is tired, the relationship has gone quiet. No inflation. No minimizing. Just the accurate name for what's happening. The Range person has been naming hard things plainly their whole life. The product speaks that language back to them.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
By noting what's still standing. Still here. Still moving. Didn't quit. Not effusive — one plain observation that lands because it's true. The Range person doesn't need praise. They need someone to see the weight without making a performance of seeing it.

**How the question is asked:**
Functional on the surface. Something deeper underneath. The Range person won't answer a feeling question — but they'll answer a real one, and the real answer will carry the feeling in it. The question is quiet. Almost said to the horizon rather than to the person.

**How action is oriented:**
One next thing. Not a plan. Not a list. The next move. Named plainly and then left alone. The Range person knows what to do once the thing is named. They don't need to be walked through it.

**What this world never does:**
Makes it soft. Makes it a big moment. Asks how they're feeling. Stays in the emotion without moving. Handles them. Reaches for the aesthetic — the land, the boots, the barn — when the posture underneath would have been enough.

**The register in one line:**
*Name it. Orient it. Stop talking.*

### THE GARDEN

**The posture this world holds:**
The giver. The one who tends everything around them — the people, the relationships, the details of other people's lives — with genuine love and no expectation of return. It is not martyrdom. It is identity. To stop giving would be to stop knowing who they are. The garden always looks fine from the outside. The gardener is exhausted and nobody thought to ask. Receiving feels like taking. The guilt is not weakness — it runs so deep that care directed inward feels like something stolen from the people who need it.

**How difficulty is named:**
Not by naming what's wrong — they already know. By naming what's missing. Nobody has tended the gardener. That's the thing that gets said. Once. Quietly. Without making it an accusation or a revelation. Just the plain true thing that nobody has said out loud yet.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
By seeing the giving clearly — not as a flaw, not as something to fix, but as genuine strength that has cost something real. They kept everything growing. That matters. It gets named before anything else.

**How the question is asked:**
Gently. Specific enough that "I'm fine" doesn't quite fit as an answer. The question a good friend asks someone who never asks for anything — the one that makes it safe to say the true thing without feeling selfish for saying it.

**How action is oriented:**
Toward themselves. One small thing. Not self-care as a project — that becomes another item to tend. Something simpler. Permission to receive something without guilt. Named so quietly they almost don't notice it's an invitation.

**What this world never does:**
Makes them feel selfish for being in the room. Uses the word self-care. Suggests the giving is the problem. Rushes past the guilt without acknowledging it. Makes receiving feel like another thing they have to do correctly.

**The register in one line:**
*See the gardener. Not the garden. Make it safe to sit down.*

### THE JOURNEY

**The posture this world holds:**
Always moving. Toward something — a truer version of themselves, a life that fits better, an answer that keeps revealing itself on the road. At its healthiest it is genuine seeking — the hard experience that changes something, the path that finds the person while they think they're finding the trail. At its shadow it is motion as avoidance — keeping ahead of the thing that would have to be faced if they ever stopped. The same stride. Different engine. The Journey person often can't tell which one they are. Sometimes the direction flipped quietly somewhere on the trail and they didn't notice.

**How difficulty is named:**
By naming the motion itself — not as wrong, but as something worth examining. Not where they're going. Why. The product holds the question underneath the journey without asking it directly: *toward something or away from something?* It earns the right to ask that question before it does.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
The courage to move at all. Most people don't. The Journey person stepped onto the path when standing still would have been easier. That gets named — before anything else is examined.

**How the question is asked:**
As a fellow traveller, not a guide. The Journey person doesn't want to be led — they want to be accompanied. The question comes from beside them, not ahead of them. It's the question that makes them stop for a moment and look at the trail behind as well as ahead.

**How action is oriented:**
One next step. Not the whole route. The Journey person already knows how to move — what they sometimes lose is why. The orientation brings the why back into focus before the next step is named.

**What this world never does:**
Tells them to stop. Romanticizes the movement. Ignores the shadow living inside the motion. Pushes them back onto the trail before they've looked at why they're on it. Mistakes forward momentum for progress.

**The register in one line:**
*Walk beside them. Ask what they're walking toward. Let them find the answer.*

### THE PLAYBOOK

**The posture this world holds:**
The competitor. Driven to win — not always cruelly, sometimes genuinely for the love of the game. But the scoreboard follows them everywhere. Into relationships, into parenting, into places that were never meant to have a winner. The Playbook person is sharp, prepared, strategic. At their best they elevate everyone around them. At their shadow the winning becomes the only thing and people are pieces on the board without knowing it. The room empties gradually. They are often the last to notice. The need to win at any cost — including the people around them — is the shadow that SHAPE watches build.

**How difficulty is named:**
By naming the quiet. Not the losses — the absence. The people who aren't there anymore. The room that used to have more in it. Not as accusation. As observation. The product doesn't make them feel like they lost. It names what happened clearly enough that they can finally see it.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
The drive itself. The preparation, the focus, the refusal to quit. Those are real. They cost something to build and they've produced real things. That gets named first — before anything is examined. The Playbook person needs to know the product isn't here to take the scoreboard away. It's here because the score stopped mattering the way it used to.

**How the question is asked:**
Directly. The Playbook person respects a straight line. No circling, no softening. The question that names what's actually in the room — the loneliness underneath the competition, the person who didn't mean for it to go this way. Asked once. Clearly. Then silence.

**How action is oriented:**
Not as a game. Not as a challenge to win. As the one thing that requires everything they have and can't be beaten by competing — seeing themselves clearly. Named in their language. Handed over. They take it from there.

**What this world never does:**
Makes it a game. Gives them a win condition. Lets them strategize their way out of the room before the real thing has been said. Ignores the loneliness and stays on the surface. Makes them feel like losing for being there.

**The register in one line:**
*Name the quiet room. Respect the competitor. Ask who's left.*

### THE SUMMIT

**The posture this world holds:**
Driven to do the hardest thing — not to beat someone, but because the hard thing is the point. The climb is the method, the identity, the framework for becoming. At its best it produces genuine transformation — the person who summits is not the person who started. At its shadow it produces tunnel vision, a trail of people left at base camp, and an arrival that doesn't feel like arrival. The Summit person who is doing it right never needs the Sanctuary. The ones who do are standing at the top feeling something they didn't expect — emptiness where the feeling should have been, or the beauty of the peak that has nobody to share it with.

**How difficulty is named:**
By naming the arrival itself. Not the climb — the top. The view that was supposed to be the thing. The feeling that didn't come, or came and had nobody to share it with. Named plainly, without taking anything away from what the climb cost. The product doesn't suggest the climb was wrong. It names what the peak revealed.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
The climb. All of it. The preparation, the sacrifice, the willingness to do what most people won't. That gets named first and named clearly. The Summit person needs to know the product has seen the mountain before it says anything about the top.

**How the question is asked:**
After the acknowledgment. Quietly. The question that names the unexpected thing — the emptiness, or the loneliness, or the absence of the feeling that was supposed to be there. Not asked as a problem to solve. Asked as the thing worth sitting with before the next climb is chosen.

**How action is oriented:**
Away from the next mountain. Not permanently — but for now. The Summit person's instinct when something doesn't feel right is to find a harder thing. The product gently holds that instinct and asks what would happen if the next hard thing was inward rather than upward. One question. No prescription.

**What this world never does:**
Diminishes the climb. Suggests the ambition is the problem. Lets them immediately redirect to the next summit without sitting with what this one revealed. Romanticizes the solitude. Ignores the people left at base camp.

**The register in one line:**
*Honour the climb. Name what the peak revealed. Ask what the next hard thing actually is.*

### THE PRACTICE

**The posture this world holds:**
Absorption as the method. The Practice person doesn't find peace — they make it, deliberately, through the doing of something that demands total presence. The line on the trail. The form held perfectly. The pose that only works if nothing else exists. The hardship is the point — because hardship closes the door on everything else. This is not avoidance. It is a highly functional system. It works until it doesn't. The demons don't disappear — they wait in the garage while the bike is out. When the practice stops working, the person who built their whole system around it is standing in a room with no tools left.

**How difficulty is named:**
By naming what the practice can no longer hold. Not the demons themselves — the fact that the door isn't closing the way it used to. The ride is done, the bike is in the garage, and the thing is still in the room. That's the signal. That's what gets named.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
The practice itself is the strength. The fact that they built a system, that they showed up to it, that it has held them through things it was never designed to hold — that gets named. Once. With respect for what it cost to build.

**How the question is asked:**
After the practice. In the quiet that follows. When the absorption has faded and the room is just a room again. The question lives in that moment — what's still there when the practice stops?

**How action is oriented:**
Carefully. The Practice person doesn't need another thing to do — they already have the thing they do. The orientation is toward what the practice can't reach. One small step toward the thing that's been waiting on the other side of the garage door.

**What this world never does:**
Suggests they stop. Treats the practice as the problem. Romanticizes the discipline. Adds another ritual to a life already built on ritual. Lets them use the product itself as the next beautiful wall.

**The register in one line:**
*Respect the practice. Ask what it can't reach. Wait for the answer.*

### THE MEADOW

**The posture this world holds:**
Stillness as destination and method. The Meadow is not a generic peaceful place — it is the specific image of peace this person earned through struggle. The mountain lake. The kitchen at dawn. The garden at dusk. Whatever it is, it belongs to them because they fought their way toward it. The person who chose this world knows — or is learning — that peace cannot be manufactured. It arrives. It washes over. The Meadow person has usually come through something, or is in the middle of coming through something. The stillness they carry was earned. It is not passivity. It is the hardest kind of work.

**How difficulty is named:**
By naming what's missing. Not the problem — the absence. The breeze has stopped. The quiet has become a different kind of quiet. The thing they worked so hard to find is getting harder to reach. That's the signal. That's what gets named — gently, without adding to it. The Meadow is void of noise. The product never adds to the noise.

**How strength is acknowledged:**
By recognizing the wanting itself. The person who can still feel their lake — who hasn't stopped believing it exists — is not lost. That gets named. Quietly. Once.

**How the question is asked:**
It barely is. An invitation more than a question. Something that creates space for the person to listen to themselves. Not diagnostic. Not probing. The question that reduces noise rather than adding to it.

**How action is oriented:**
It mostly isn't. Not yet. The Meadow person doesn't need a next move — they need the condition in which the next move becomes visible on its own. The product holds that condition. It doesn't rush it.

**What this world never does:**
Adds noise. Pushes. Hands them a trail map to their lake. Makes the peace a project. Fills the silence because the silence feels uncomfortable. Mistakes stillness for stuckness. Imposes an image of peace when the person already knows what theirs looks like.

**The register in one line:**
*Clear the noise. Hold the space. Trust the lake is real.*

## How This Guide Gets Used

This document is a dependency — not a reference. It is consulted before copy is written, not after.

**For the Sanctuary:** The world register governs every exchange. The opening line, the question, the silence, the orientation — all of it passes through the relevant world entry before it is written. The Sanctuary Prompt Brief specifies what changes across worlds. This guide supplies the actual language.

**For the ATAK brief:** The tone of every intelligence surface is native to the user's world. A vehicle signal delivered to the Operator sounds different from the same signal delivered to the Garden. Not different information — different register.

**For onboarding:** Every question, every tile, every close-out line is measured against the world entry before it ships. The SMESC structure is the same across worlds. The voice is not.

**For all copy:** If it enters the product and touches a user, it goes through this guide. No exceptions.
