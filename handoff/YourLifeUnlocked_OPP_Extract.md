## 1. What SMESC Is

**SMESC** is the five-part structure of a Canadian Forces Operation Order (Op O). It stands for:

| Letter | Element | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Situation | The current state of the environment — friendly forces, enemy forces, context |
| **M** | Mission | The task to be accomplished and the purpose behind it |
| **E** | Execution | How the mission will be carried out — the Commander's intent, concept of operations, tasks to subordinates |
| **S** | Service Support | Logistics — what resources are available and how sustainment works |
| **C** | Command & Signals | Who is in command, succession of command, communications |

This is the backbone of every Canadian Army operation order, from platoon level to brigade group. It is taught at the Canadian Army Command and Staff College and is the standard format across Canadian Land Operations doctrine.

---

## 2. The Structure in Doctrine

From *B-GL-331-002/FP-001, Staff Duties for Land Operations* (referenced in the OPP Handbook):

The Op O follows the SMESC format. Each paragraph has a defined purpose:

- **Situation** establishes the context: what the enemy is doing, what friendly forces are doing, and any other factors affecting the operation.
- **Mission** is a single, clear statement of what must be accomplished and why — the "what" and "purpose."
- **Execution** is the largest paragraph: it contains the Commander's Intent, the Concept of Operations (CONOPS), tasks to each subordinate unit, and coordinating instructions.
- **Service Support** covers all logistic and administrative matters — ammunition, fuel, medical, maintenance, movement.
- **Command & Signals** states the command structure, succession of command if the commander becomes a casualty, and all communications and reporting requirements.

---

## 3. The Commander's Intent

The Commander's Intent is embedded within the **Execution** paragraph and is considered the single most important element of any order. It states:

- The **end state** — what things must look like when the mission is complete
- The **key tasks** — the two or three things that absolutely must happen
- The **main effort** — where the Commander wants decisive action focused

*"It is imperative that all planners know and understand their Commander's intent before they begin planning."* — OPP Handbook, p.12

The intent gives subordinates the freedom to act independently when the plan breaks down. If a soldier knows the Commander's Intent, they can make decisions without waiting for orders.

## 4. The OPP as a Cycle (Relevant Principle)

The OPP is never finished. Once a plan is executed, monitoring begins immediately. If the plan no longer reflects reality, a new cycle is triggered. This is called **Plan Review** (Stage 5).

The principle that applies to Your Life / Unlocked:

> *"Never fall in love with your plan: even though it may have seemed like a very good plan when you wrote it, it may have to be changed radically at short notice."* — OPP Handbook, p.12

This mirrors the app's **Seasonal Intelligence Principle** — the app never locks a user into a fixed profile. It monitors, detects drift, and adapts.

## 5. How SMESC Maps to the Operator Onboarding

The Operator season uses SMESC as its onboarding scaffold. Each element maps directly:

| SMESC Element | Operator Question | What the App Learns |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation (S)** | *"Who's on your team?"* | Household composition |
| **Mission (M)** | *"What's the gap?"* | Primary problem space, what's being left unmanaged |
| **Execution (E)** | *"You'll get what you need, when you need it."* | The Restraint Principle — delivered as a statement, not a question |
| **Service Support (S)** | *"What are you responsible for keeping operational?"* | Logistical / maintenance domains the user owns |
| **Command & Signals (C)** | *"You're always in command. We'll find our rhythm."* | The relationship agreement — authority stays with the user |

The Operator season close-out — *"You have the intelligence. You have the mission. Move out."* — directly echoes the handoff moment in a real operation order, where the Commander transfers authority to subordinate commanders to execute.
